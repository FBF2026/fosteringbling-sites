Know Your Rights

Illinois foster parents have legal rights. Know them before your next call or visit.

🏠 Right to Advance Notice

You have the right to advance notice before a placement is removed, except in emergencies involving child safety. DCFS must give you an opportunity to be heard.

📋 Right to Information

You have the right to receive information about the child's background, health history, and educational needs prior to or at the time of placement.

💬 Right to Be Heard

You have the right to be notified of and participate in court proceedings involving the child in your care and to provide input to the court.

🤝 Right to Services

You have the right to receive training, support services, and assistance from your licensing agency to help meet the needs of children in your care.

⚖️ Right to Fair Treatment

You have the right to be free from arbitrary or capricious decisions. If you believe a decision is unfair, you have the right to request a review or fair hearing.

📞 Right to Contact an Ombudsman

Illinois has a Foster Care Ombudsman office you can contact if you have concerns about DCFS decisions. This is separate from your caseworker.

📄 Right to Documentation

You have the right to receive copies of all reports, assessments, and documentation related to children in your care and to your licensing.

🔒 Right to Privacy

Your home and family have privacy rights. DCFS must have a valid reason to conduct unannounced visits and must follow proper procedures.

Escalation Scripts

Ready-to-use scripts for difficult situations — or generate a custom one for your specific situation.

🚨 When DCFS says they're removing the child with no notice
▶
"I want to make sure I understand the situation correctly. I'm requesting to be informed of the specific reason for this removal and whether an emergency is involved. Under Illinois foster parent rights, I have the right to advance notice and the opportunity to be heard unless there is an immediate safety threat. Can you please confirm the reason and the timeline? I would also like this conversation documented. If you are proceeding today, I am requesting the name and direct contact of your supervisor."
📞 When caseworker won't return your calls
▶
"Hello, this is [your name], licensed foster parent for [child's name]. I have attempted to reach [caseworker name] on [dates] without response. I am documenting this communication attempt as required. I need [specific request — e.g. medical authorization / information about upcoming court date]. I am now reaching out to [supervisor name] to ensure this matter is addressed promptly. Please return my call by [specific date] at [phone number]. Thank you."
🏠 When a caseworker shows up unannounced
▶
"Hello. I want to be cooperative and I'm happy to work with you. I do want to note the time of your arrival for my records. Could you please state your name, agency, and the purpose of this visit? I want to make sure everything is properly documented on my end. I'll invite you in — please give me just a moment."
⚖️ When you're responding to an allegation
▶
"I want to fully cooperate with this process and I take this seriously. I am requesting to be informed in writing of the specific allegation so I can respond appropriately. I want it on record that I dispute [specific claim] and I have documentation to support my account. I am requesting the name of the investigator and the expected timeline. I also want to note that I have been [child's name]'s foster parent for [length of time] and have [relevant context]. I am contacting my licensing worker to ensure I have appropriate support through this process."
